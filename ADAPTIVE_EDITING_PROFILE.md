# Adaptive Editing Profile

One all-in-one profile for long-form YouTube, vlogs, Shorts, Reels, product/affiliate videos, and mini-dramas.

## Signature
Premium story-first editing: purposeful motion graphics, cinematic or energetic pacing selected by format, polished transitions, contextual VFX, layered SFX/Foley/ambience, consistent typography, and an audited final render. The shared brand should feel consistent, but the effect density must adapt to the content.

## Format adaptation
- **Long-form YouTube:** 16:9 by default; chapter-based story flow, rich graphics, data/diagram reveals, B-roll, source/context inserts, layered audio, visual refreshes through narration-heavy sections.
- **Vlogs:** creator personality first; location/context graphics, animated lower thirds, maps when supported, playful accents, real ambience, and natural pacing.
- **Shorts/Reels:** 9:16 by default; immediate hook, active captions for spoken explainers, faster visual changes, strong callouts, meaningful transition/SFX accents, and platform-safe framing.
- **Mini-drama:** protect performance, continuity, emotional holds, clean dialogue subtitles, cinematic motivated transitions, story-native graphics, and expressive sound design; avoid generic floating explainer cards over acting.
- **Product/affiliate:** show the real product clearly; animate accurate feature labels and comparisons, sync Foley to demonstrations, and never invent product claims.

## Visual treatment
Use supplied channel branding if available. If not, use graphite `#111318`, off-white `#F5F7FA`, signal lime `#C5FF3D`, and cyan `#58D5FF` for informational graphics as a starting palette. Keep fiction and natural vlogs visually appropriate to their own scenes.

Preferred design elements include kinetic titles, animated lower thirds, callouts, number/stat/chart builds, diagrams, timelines, maps, source cards, split screens, picture-in-picture, freeze-frame annotations where reliable, kinetic captions, chapter bumpers, end cards, and motion-staged text reveals. Use actual facts/assets only. Keep typography, spacing, easing, color, and transition behavior consistent.

## Editing and sound rules
- Clean cuts are the default. Transitions must be motivated by action, camera movement, sound, mood, time, or information.
- Use punch-ins, speed ramps, masks, motion blur, shake, grain, glow, and glitch only when appropriate and supported by the installed tools.
- Build a clear sound hierarchy: dialogue/production audio first, then Foley/SFX, then music/ambience. Duck music under dialogue based on actual levels; do not rely on one fixed volume value.
- Search the local SFX catalog before using a sound file. Never invent filenames, stats, prices, quotes, or graphic data.
- Preserve emotional pauses and meaningful ambience. Do not put an SFX on every cut or every word.

## Delivery QC
Check aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, duration, audio mapping, sync, captions, graphics safe zones, off-screen text, transition artifacts, and voice/music balance. Deliver one polished requested master with subtitle sidecars when appropriate; only create extra variants when asked. Be transparent about effects or checks blocked by missing assets/tools.
